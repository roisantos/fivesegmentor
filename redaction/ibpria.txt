Abstract. Retinal fundus imaging offers a noninvasive window into the
eyes microvasculature, critical for early detection of both ocular and systemic diseases. In this work, we introduce VesselView, a U-Netinspired
convolutional neural network designed for precise segmentation of retinal vessels in high-resolution fundus images. VesselView features doubleconvolution residual blocks with large kernels, a deepened bottleneck
and skip connections. We conduct a fair comparative evaluation on the
FIVES dataset at its full resolution (2048×2048), benchmarking VesselView against state-of-the-art models. The quantitative results demonstrate that VesselView achieves superior overall performanceas measured by the area under the ROC curvewith particularly strong results
in glaucomatous and normal images. The qualitative comparative provides complementary evidence to the quantitative findings by showing
that VesselView balances fewer missed vessels with more false positives
than competing models, and validates the critical role of the chosen skip
connections through an ablation study. These findings underscore the
potential of specialized deep learning architectures for high-resolution
retinal vessel segmentation.

1 Introduction
Retinal fundus images provide a noninvasive window into the internal structures of the eye, which is critical for the diagnosis of prevalent diseases such
as diabetic retinopathy (DR), glaucoma, and age-related macular degeneration
(AMD) [21, 22, 1, 16]. Furthermore, retinal vascular network often reflects early
signs of both ocular and systemic pathologies [24, 8, 9, 18]. The identification of
the corresponding morphological biomarkers (as thickness, curvature, density,
or branching patterns) is based on the segmentation of the vascular structure.
Current automatic segmentation techniques leverage the capabilities of machine
learning approaches to provide tools that improve the performance of manual
2 Roi Santos-Mateos et al.
segmentation in terms of time, objectivity and accuracy [3, 25]. The majority of
these approaches are based on the UNet model [20, 5]. Despite advances in model
architectures for segmentation, current research is predominantly fueled by standardized datasets such as DRIVE [21] (768×584 pixels, 40 images), STARE [12]
(700×605 pixels, 20 images) and CHASEDB1 [7] (990×960 pixels, 28 images)
that are small in size, lack diversity and have low resolution, which limits generalization in learning.
The new FIVES dataset [15] improves upon previous datasets by providing
images that covering a broader range of clinical conditions (DR, AMD, glaucoma, and healthy eyes), higher resolution (2048×2048), and a larger number
of annotated samples (800 distributed in 600 for training and 200 for testing).
While many recent studies have used downscaled versions of FIVES for computational efficiency [14, 6], we retain the original high resolution to preserve fine
vascular details critical for accurate segmentation.
Main contributions. We present VesselView, a U-Netinspired CNN that (i)
uses 9×9 residual blocks, a deeper bottleneck, and selective 1×1-refined skip
connections to capture fine vessels and suppress noise; (ii) is trained on FIVES
without down-sampling; and (iii) surpasses four strong baselines in AUC across
DR, AMD, glaucoma and healthy eyes, with an ablation confirming the value of
its skip strategy.
The remainder of this paper is structured as follows: Section 2 introduces
the related work, Section 3 describes our proposal, Section 4 details the dataset,
experimental methodology, and presents our results and analysis, and Section 4.4
discusses the implications of our findings for clinical practice and future research.
2 Related Work
The application of deep learning to retinal image analysis has evolved rapidly,
with CNNs proving particularly effective for vessel segmentation and disease
classification [19, 13, 25]. The U-Net architecture [20] remains foundational for
medical image segmentation due to its encoder-decoder structure with skip connections that preserve spatial details. Retinal fundus images are typically large
and high-resolution, as the vascular structure of interest presents very fine details
and low-contrast areas against cluttered backgrounds.
Hence, they are very challenging for raters and consequently, annotations
for vascular structures are sparser than for other organs and structures in the
biomedical domain [2, 4]. To address such issues, the WNet [10] was proposed.
It is a dual-path fully convolutional network, similar to a double U-Net, with
a postprocessing step that combines conditional random field smoothing and
hierarchical merging, yielding competitive segmentation results. The intuition
behind the two stacked U-Nets is that the first U-Net produces an initial segmentation approximation, which is then used as an attention-like map to refine
the segmentation in the second U-Net.
SA-UNet [11] is a spatial attention U-Net proposed to circumvent the requirement of massive training datasets. Using a spatial attention module and

structured dropout convolutional blocks, instead of the original U-Net convolutional blocks, it focuses on the most informative pixels to retain the relevant fine
details in the vasculature while avoiding overfitting.
Other architectures like FR-UNet [17] tackles the flaws in detecting fine vessels by enhancing U-Net-based architectures with a multi-resolution interactive
mechanism. While U-Net increases receptive field by downsampling, FR-UNet
maintains full resolution through the entire process for precise segmentation,
while a feature aggregation module fuses features from different scales to conjugate small-capillary sensitivity with contextual awareness. Additionally, a dualthreshold iterative algorithm leverages pixel neighborhood relationships to refine
vessel detection and connectivity in the final binary segmentation map.
A common limitation in the learning process of previous methods is that
they have been trained predominantly on small datasets, such as DRIVE [21],
STARE [12], and CHASEDB1 [7], which lack the variability found in clinical
practice. Previous work with multi-disease datasets has highlighted domain shift
challenges, with models often showing performance degradation when applied to
images outside their training distribution [10, 23, 15].
Unlike WNets two-stage refinement, SA-UNets attention focus, or FR-UNets
full-resolution streams, VesselView fuses large-kernel residual blocks, a deep bottleneck, and 1×1-refined skip linksan as-yet untried mix that drives its edge
on FIVES.
3 Proposed Segmentation Network
In this paper we introduce VesselView, a U-Net-like deep convolutional neural
network specifically engineered for the precise semantic segmentation of color
retinal fundus images, with a primary focus on delineating intricate blood vessel
structures. The architecture, as showed in Figure 1, is built on an encoderdecoder framework enriched with strategic skip connections and a deepened
bottleneck, utilizing advanced residual building blocks to robustly extract and
integrate local and global features, which is critical for capturing complex vascular patterns in high-resolution retinal imagery. This design addresses the unique
challenges of retinal fundus imaging, such as noise, fine vessel delineation, and
subtle pathological features, positioning VesselView as an optimized tool for
automated vessel segmentation in clinical ophthalmology.

3.2 VesselView Architecture
VesselView is built upon a U-Net-like encoder-decoder framework but introduces
several modifications tailored for segmenting retinal vessels in high-resolution
fundus images. These enhancements address challenges such as noise, fine vessel
delineation, and computational efficiency, making VesselView distinct from the
standard U-Net.
Encoder Pathway The encoder extracts hierarchical features from RGB retinal fundus images with an input resolution of H × W. Unlike U-Nets standard
3 × 3 convolutions, VesselView uses residual block-like convolutions with 9 × 9
kernels to capture broader spatial contexts critical for vascular networks. The
process starts with an initial residual block that produces a feature map with
3. PROPOSED SEGMENTATION NETWORK 5
32 filters at full resolution (H, W). A second residual block increases this to
64 filters, still at H, W. Downsampling occurs in two stages: first, max-pooling
is followed by an explicit 1 × 1 convolutiona feature not found in U-Net
reducing the resolution to H/2, W/2 and increasing the channels to 128 filters.
This output is saved as a skip connection. The second stage further reduces the
resolution to H/4, W/4 with 256 filters, also saved as a skip connection.
Bottleneck The bottleneck in VesselView is deepened compared to U-Nets
single convolutional layer, featuring two residual blocks with 256 channels at
H/4, W/4. This design strengthens the extraction of high-level semantic features
vital for segmenting thin vessels. The output of the second residual block is
concatenated with the 256-channel skip connection from the encoder, creating a
512-channel feature map. A 3 × 3 convolution, batch normalization, and ReLU
activation then refine this to 256 channels, maintaining the H/4, W/4 resolution.
Decoder Pathway The decoder reconstructs the spatial resolution and refines
segmentation predictions. Starting from the bottlenecks 256-channel output at
H/4, W/4, a residual block reduces this to 128 channels. A transposed 2 × 2
convolution upsamples the feature map to H/2, W/2 with 64 channels, which
is then concatenated with the 128-channel skip connection from the encoder,
forming a 192-channel feature map. This is refined to 64 channels via a 3 × 3
convolution, batch normalization, and ReLU activation. A second residual block
processes this at H/2, W/2, followed by another transposed 2×2 convolution that
upsamples to full resolution (H, W) with 32 channels. This is concatenated with
the 32-channel skip connection from the encoders initial block, yielding a 64-
channel feature map, refined to 32 channels. A final residual block processes this,
and a 1×1 convolution with sigmoid activation generates the segmentation mask.
VesselViews skip connection strategy differs from U-Nets direct concatenation
by selectively using skip connections (e.g., post-1 × 1 convolution outputs) and
refining them with convolutions after merging with upsampled features.
Dataset Preparation Three channels were used as input for the retinal fundus images and one channel for the ground truth segmentation masks. During
training, data augmentation strategies (random rotations, flips) were applied to
increase the diversity of training samples and mitigate overfitting.
Data augmentation: Training images were augmented using geometric transformations with a 50% probability: Rotations were applied with fixed angles of
-180°, -90°, 0°, or 90°. Independently, horizontal and vertical flips were applied.
These augmentations were implemented with linear interpolation for the image
and nearest-neighbor interpolation for the label. On-the-fly sampling means the
disk dataset stays at 600 images (H 150 per class), but with the 12.5% chance
of no transform any given pass, each epoch introduces H 525 novel variants,
totalling H 105 k augmented samples over 200 epochs.

4 Experiments and Results
All experiments were conducted on FIVES dataset [15]. The original image size
of 2048×2048 pixels and test partition were preserved. All models were trained
using Dice loss function, Adam optimizer with an initial learning rate of 1×104
,
weight decay of 1 × 103
, a batch size of 1, and a total of 200 epochs (tuned
based on validation performance). In particular, for the baseline U-Net [20] its
ResNet34 encoder was pretrained on the ImageNet dataset. Data augmentation
(random rotations, flips, and brightness adjustments) was applied to improve
model generalization. All models were trained on 80% of the "train" partition
of the FIVES dataset, with the remaining 20% used for validation.
To comprehensively evaluate our approach, we compare VesselView against
several established state-of-the-art networks, namely U-Net using an ResNet34
encoder pre-trained in ImageNet [20], WNet [10], SA-UNet [11], and FR-UNet [17].
Models were trained using Adam.
Evaluation Metrics We evaluated segmentation performance using standard
metrics: Accuracy (proportion of correctly segmented pixels), Sensitivity or Recall (fraction of vessel pixels correctly identified), Specificity (fraction of background pixels correctly identified), Precision (fraction of predicted vessel pixels
that are correct), F1 (Dice) Score (harmonic mean of Precision and Sensitivity),
Intersection over Union (IoU) (overlap between prediction and ground truth),
Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC) (correlation between predicted and
actual binary classifications), and Area Under the ROC Curve (AUC) (models
discriminative ability across thresholds). In the FIVES dataset, some thin vessels are missing from the Ground Truth, causing sensitivity and Dice metrics
to penalize models for detecting these unlabeled vessels, thus underestimating
true segmentation performance. AUC, however, evaluates the trade-off between
sensitivity and false positive rate across all thresholds, focusing on the models
discriminative ability rather than exact alignment with the ground truth. This
threshold-independency property makes AUC more resilient when the ground
truth is incomplete, as it doesnt punish the model as harshly as Dice for detecting real but unlabeled features. Given the clinical need to detect fine vessels
for early disease diagnosis, we argue that AUC is a more suitable metric for
assessing vessel segmentation in this context.

4.3 Ablation Study
To assess the role of skip connections in preserving fine vascular details, we compared the original VesselView architecture with a variant where skip connections
are entirely removed (termed NoSkip). In this variant, the encoder and decoder
operate without lateral feature fusion, meaning that high-resolution information
from early encoder layers is not reintroduced during upsampling. Consequently,
the NoSkip variant relies solely on the bottleneck output and the decoders upsampling pathway to reconstruct vessel structures.

4.4 Discussion
VesselViews superior AUC make it particularly valuable for screening applications, where detecting all potential vessel abnormalities takes precedence over
precise boundary delineation. Its varying performance across disease groups suggests targeted improvements could enhance clinical utility, particularly for glaucoma cases.
The disease-specific findings reveal critical insights for model selection in clinical and research settings. VesselViews robust performance, especially in Glaucoma, positions it as a promising tool for screening applications where detecting
all vesselsparticularly in subtle disease statesis paramount. Conversely, UNet with pretrained ResNet34 encoder shows strength in boundary delineation,
which makes it ideal for applications requiring high precision, such as quantitative vessel analysis across multiple conditions. The weaker performance of
SA-UNet and WNet in sensitivity highlights their limitations for disease-specific

tasks, while FR-UNets consistency may suit general-purpose use without excelling in any particular area. Ultimately, these results emphasize the importance
of tailoring model choice to the target disease, as performance varies significantly
across retinal conditions.
5 Conclusions
We present VesselView, a specialized U-Net-like architecture for high-resolution
retinal vessel segmentation featuring double convolutional residual block operations, a deepened bottleneck, and strategic skip connections. Evaluated on the
FIVES dataset against established models, VesselView demonstrates superior
AUC, critical metrics for clinical screening applications. Disease-specific analysis has revealed excellent performance. The qualitative results show more segmented vessels than its closest competitor, but they are penalized by more false
positives. The ablation study highlights the importance of skip connections in
the segmentation performance of VesselView. These results highlight the importance of task-specific network design and high-quality, diverse data sets to
advance retinal vessel segmentation in clinical applications. Future work will
explore loss functions, attention mechanisms, and architectural refinements to
address disease-specific challenges.
